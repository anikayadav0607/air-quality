3. Key Environmental Observations
  1. Industrial Dominance: Industrial Zone North logged the highest annual mean concentrations for both PM10 (150.2 µg/m³) and PM2.5 (87.6 µg/m³), frequently crossing the 24-hour safe limits established by environmental authorities.
  2. Particulate Correlation: Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) displays a strong linear correlation with coarse particulate matter (PM10) with an estimated correlation coefficient \(r = 0.88\), indicating common vehicular and industrial combustion origins.
  3. Seasonal Inversion Peaks: Severe spikes in fine particulate readings occur in late November and December due to atmospheric thermal inversion trapping pollutants close to the ground.
  4. Summer Ground Ozone: Ground-level Ozone reaches seasonal highs during July (79.5 ppb) triggered by solar ultraviolet catalysis of precursor volatile organics and nitrogen oxides.

Project Purpose & Scope

The Air Quality and Pollution Pattern Analysis (DA-013) system analyses documented public air-quality measurements to compare pollutant levels over time and across monitoring locations. It is designed to assist environmental science students, municipal data analysts, and researchers in identifying severe pollution episodes, evaluating spatial gradients, and exploring statistical distributions among key atmospheric species (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, O3).

Scientific & Analytical Disclaimer

"This application presents descriptive analysis of recorded measurements. Missing values are not treated as zero, and comparisons must account for measurement units, monitoring locations, and data quality. The results do not establish pollution causes or provide medical conclusions."
